Part 7 · Classification

Four criteria, four groups: who needs attention first

Each criterion answers a question a bank needs answered. Developers are grouped by the decision tree below; the “Reason” column shows which criterion pulls a firm down. The table updates when the scenario changes in Part 6.

① Shocked coverage ratio

Enough cash to service 12 months of debt?

≥ 1.2× · 1.0× · < 1.0×

Basis: Basel III LCR logic (sources ≥ needs); a 20% buffer covers the error from using gross profit instead of cash flow.

② Shock tolerance s*

How far can sales fall before a shortfall?

s* ≥ 45%

Basis: revenue fell 44.5% (median) in H1 2023 versus Q4 2022 — Part 5.

③ Ability to close the gap

Are ordinary tools enough?

sell ≤ 10% of inventory + rollover ≤ 80%

Basis: 80% is the normal-times rollover rate (“Normal” scenario); 10% is the “Self-rescue” scenario. Any remainder must be borne by shareholders/restructuring.

④ Leverage D/E

Is there room to borrow more?

≤ 1.5 · > 2.0 · legal cap 5.0

Basis: Decree 200/2026 allows private bond issuance only when liabilities ≤ 5× equity; 2.0 is the level beyond which borrowing more is no longer sensible.

Decision tree. Coverage ≥ 1 → A · Resilient if coverage ≥ 1.2×, s* ≥ 45% and D/E ≤ 1.5 all hold; otherwise → B · Watch. Coverage < 1 → C · Needs liquidity support if the gap can be closed with ③ and D/E ≤ 2; otherwise → D · High stress (at risk of not fully repaying maturing debt if the stress scenario occurs — this does not mean insolvency).
VHM: “short-term liabilities” include buyer prepayments (settled by handing over homes, not with cash), so VHM’s group is an upper bound on its risk. Data separating short-term borrowings (code 320) is needed to conclude.
